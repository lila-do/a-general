Welcome

Why Hydra is special

Hydra is one of the best-known islands of the Saronic Gulf, close enough to Athens to be reached easily by sea. Its town rises amphitheatrically around a picturesque harbour, with stone mansions climbing the rocky slopes. One of its most unusual features is the absence of ordinary cars and motorcycles: visitors move mainly on foot, while donkeys, mules and boats remain part of everyday island transport.

Language-learning focus: This guide uses the information from the Hydra project text as the basis for its history and comprehension activities, while adding current visitor information for transport, accommodation and sightseeing.
01 • Where is Hydra?

Location

Argosaronic / Saronic Gulf

Hydra is a Greek island in the Saronic Gulf, south of Athens and a relatively short sea journey from the port of Piraeus. The main town and harbour are on the island’s northern side.

The landscape is rocky and mountainous, with stone-built settlements, coastal paths and views over the Aegean.

Name & landscape

The school project text explains that Hydra was once said to have been covered with forests and that its name means a place with plenty of water. Today, however, much of the island appears grey, dry and rocky.

02 • From a dry island to a maritime power

Hydra’s history — based on the project text

Early settlement
Because agriculture was difficult on the infertile island, the first inhabitants turned towards the sea and began building ships.
1715
According to the text, when the Turks captured Hydra, they paid little attention to the infertile island.
18th century
The Hydriots developed a strong fleet and gradually became an important merchant-marine community.
By the 19th century
Hydra had become wealthy and powerful at sea. The project text states that it had acquired autonomous status and that Hydriot ships held a monopoly in Mediterranean food supplies.
1821
The Hydriots were not the first to begin the Greek War of Independence, but when they joined, their ships made up a very large part of the Greek revolutionary fleet. The text states that Hydriot ships represented two-thirds of the whole Greek fleet.
Revolution
Hydra also offered its people and resources. Admiral Andreas Miaoulis became a famous commander of the Greek fleet. The school text also presents Georgios Koundouriotis as a major political figure in the newly emerging Greek state.

03 • What to see

Places of interest

Essential visit

Historical Archives – Museum of Hydra

The museum is central to the school text. Its collections connect visitors with Hydra’s maritime and revolutionary history. Exhibits include Hydriot costumes, objects from the War of Independence, historical documents, maps, navigation instruments, carved wooden ship figureheads, ship models, lithographs and paintings connected with the ships and fighters of 1821.

The museum also preserves the embalmed heart of Admiral Andreas Miaoulis.

Photo: Macedon-40 / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 3.0.
Historic mansion

Lazaros Koundouriotis Historical Mansion

Built during Hydra’s period of naval prosperity, the mansion stands on the western side of the harbour. It is connected with one of the island’s important political and shipping families and today functions as a museum.

Harbour

Hydra Town & harbour

Walk along the waterfront and through the narrow stone lanes. The harbour is the visual and social centre of the island and reflects Hydra’s long relationship with shipping.

Kaminia

The Red House / Miaoulis residence

The former residence of Andreas Miaoulis at the small harbour of Kaminia dates from 1786 and is associated with exhibitions and Hydra’s artistic life.

Religious heritage

Monastery of Prophet Elias

A rewarding destination for walkers. The island’s paths also provide wide views of the rocky landscape and sea.

Contemporary culture

Art spaces

Hydra also has a strong modern art identity, with venues such as the Melina Mercouri Cultural Hall, the DESTE Foundation project space and the Tompazis Mansion.

04 • Where to stay

Accommodation

Hydra offers small hotels, guesthouses, apartments and traditional stone houses. The best area depends on how close you want to be to the harbour and how much walking you are comfortable with.

Hydra Town / Harbour

Best for first-time visitors, ferry access, cafés, museums and evening walks. It is usually the most convenient choice if you are carrying luggage.

Kaminia

A quieter area west of the main harbour, still within walking distance for many visitors. Good for a more relaxed atmosphere.

Further from the port

Traditional rooms and houses can offer peace and views, but remember that Hydra is steep in places and ordinary car transfers are not available.

Before booking: check the exact distance from the port, the number of steps, luggage-transfer arrangements, cancellation rules and current prices.
05 • Getting there & moving around

Transport

From Athens / Piraeus

High-speed passenger ferries operate between Piraeus and Hydra. Journey times and frequencies depend on the service and season, so travellers should check the current timetable before departure.

Via the Peloponnese

Another option is to travel by road from Athens towards the eastern Peloponnese and Metochi, then continue to Hydra by ferry, boat or water taxi. Visit Greece lists the road journey to Metochi at about 2 hours 25 minutes before the sea crossing.

Hydra’s car-free transport culture. Photo: Sharon Mollerus / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 2.0.

On the island

On foot

Walking is the main way to explore Hydra Town and many nearby areas. Wear comfortable shoes: streets and paths can be steep.

Donkeys & mules

Pack animals remain part of Hydra’s traditional transport system, especially for moving goods and luggage.

Water taxis & boats

Boats connect the harbour with coastal areas and can be useful for beaches or locations that are inconvenient to reach on foot.

What makes Hydra unusual? Ordinary motor vehicles are prohibited, so there is no normal car-and-motorcycle traffic in Hydra Town. This is one reason the island feels very different from many other tourist destinations.
06 • Memory and celebration

Miaoulia

The school project text describes Miaoulia as a major festival held in Hydra around 20 June in memory of Admiral Andreas Miaoulis. It describes a Greek Air Force plane dropping a wreath into the sea as part of the commemoration.

The text ends by encouraging visitors to “communicate with history”: it points to Miaoulis’ cross and, most importantly, his preserved heart in the Historical Archives – Museum of Hydra as a powerful connection between the present and the island’s revolutionary past.

07 • Reading comprehension

True or False? — The Hydra Project

Answer from the information in the project text. Tap each statement to reveal the answer and explanation.

1. Hydra has always been a dry island with no forests.
FALSE

The text says Hydra was once said to have been covered with forests.

2. Because agriculture was impossible, people started building ships.
TRUE

The text links the island’s poor agricultural possibilities with the development of shipbuilding.

3. The island could offer a lot, and that’s why the Turks settled on the island.
FALSE

The text says the Turks ignored the infertile island after capturing it in 1715.

4. The Hydriots became important merchants by the 19th century.
TRUE

The text describes Hydra as rich and a great maritime power by the 19th century.

5. Hydriots supplied the Turks with food products. They had the monopoly in the Mediterranean Sea.
FALSE

The text says Hydriot ships had a monopoly of food supplies in the Mediterranean, but it does not say that this meant supplying the Turks.

6. Hydriots were the first to start the Greek War of Independence.
FALSE

The text explicitly says they were not the first to start the Revolution.

7. The Hydriot ships made up the whole Greek fleet.
FALSE

The text says they made up two-thirds of the Greek fleet, not all of it.

8. Admiral Andreas Miaoulis became the first Prime Minister of the New Greek State.
FALSE

In the project text, Miaoulis is presented as commander of the Greek fleet, while Georgios Koundouriotis is the political figure mentioned in connection with the new state.

9. You can find figureheads and paintings of the ships of the Greek Revolution in the Museum of Hydra.
TRUE

The text specifically mentions figureheads and paintings of the revolutionary ships among the museum exhibits.

10. Nothing is left to see of Admiral Andreas Miaoulis today.
FALSE

The project text mentions Miaoulis’ cross and his preserved heart in the Museum of Hydra.
